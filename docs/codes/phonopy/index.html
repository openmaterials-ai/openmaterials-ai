What phonopy is

phonopy computes harmonic phonon properties from second-order force constants obtained by finite displacements: band structures, densities of states, thermal properties and group velocities. It reads forces from many DFT codes and potentials. On the map it serves the harmonic quantities and the quantities built from them.

In more detail

The supercell approach

Second-order force constants are the negative derivative of the force on one atom with respect to the displacement of another. phonopy displaces atoms in a supercell by a small distance, reads the forces computed by an external first-principles code, and uses crystal symmetry both to reduce the number of displaced supercells and to improve the numerical accuracy of the fit (Togo 2015). The force constants are exact only at wave vectors commensurate with the supercell and are Fourier interpolated elsewhere, so a supercell of a few hundred atoms is usual. For polar crystals the non-analytical term correction from Born charges and the dielectric tensor restores the LO-TO splitting (Togo 2023).

Phonon and thermal properties

From the dynamical matrix phonopy returns frequencies and eigenvectors on paths and meshes, total and projected densities of states, irreducible representations, group velocities and mean square displacements. The frequencies give the constant-volume thermal properties Cv, Helmholtz free energy and entropy. In the quasi-harmonic approximation a fit of the free energy at several volumes gives the Gibbs energy, the equilibrium volume, the volumetric thermal expansion and Cp. For Al the computed Cp agrees with experiment, and the gap between Cp and Cv is not negligible at high temperature (Togo 2015). Mode Gruneisen parameters come from the volume derivative of the dynamical matrix.

Instabilities and finite temperature

Imaginary modes mark dynamical instabilities. Following their eigenvectors maps phase transition pathways. For simple cubic Cu the M and X point modes lead to P4/nmm, Pmma and Cmcm structures and on to BCT, SH, FCC, BCC and HCP (Togo 2015). For strongly anharmonic crystals phonopy generates supercells with random displacements sampled from harmonic oscillators at a given temperature, valid only when all modes at commensurate q points are stable. For NaCl, 50,000 randomly displaced 512-atom supercells built from renormalized force constants give displacement distributions similar to those of an MD run on the same supercell (Togo 2023).

Force codes and automation

Forces come from the user's first-principles code. The 2023 review lists Quantum ESPRESSO, Abinit and ELK for DFPT and VASP for both approaches. It also lists the AiiDA plugins for phonopy and phono3py, which run the six-step workflow (standardize the cell, relax, displace, compute forces, fit and analyse) as an acyclic directed graph (Togo 2023). ALM and hiPhive are separate codes built for force-constant fitting (Togo 2023). Cai 2019 uses phonopy for computed thermal expansion, and Brivio 2015 for the dynamical matrix of DFT force constants.
