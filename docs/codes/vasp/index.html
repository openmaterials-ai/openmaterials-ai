What VASP is

VASP is a plane-wave electronic structure code using the projector augmented-wave method. It computes total energies, forces, stresses, electronic structure and ab initio molecular dynamics, and is licensed commercially. On the map it serves the ground-state quantities it computes.

In more detail

Self-consistency and PAW

VASP solves the Kohn-Sham equations in a plane-wave basis and reaches the ground state through a self-consistency cycle rather than direct minimization of the functional (Kresse 1996). The paper compares blocked Davidson, sequential conjugate-gradient and RMM-DIIS diagonalization and finds RMM-DIIS most efficient for large systems. Pulay's scheme mixes the charge density under a metric and preconditioner designed for plane waves. Core electrons enter through the PAW method, which splits the Hartree energy as pseudopotential plane-wave codes do and includes partial core corrections in the exchange-correlation energy on the plane-wave grid (Kresse 1999).

Numerics and scale

For metals the self-consistency cycle is significantly faster than direct minimization with conjugate gradients, and the number of iterations to a given precision is almost independent of system size (Kresse 1996). Computer time per iteration scales as the square of the number of atoms for systems up to 1000 electrons. In 1996 the program handled up to 100 transition metal atoms or 200 simple element atoms on 256 MB workstations. Cholesky-based Gram-Schmidt orthonormalization ran two to four times faster than the sequential scheme. PAW bond length errors stay below 0.5 percent even for datasets needing less than 400 eV (Kresse 1999).

Validation

Against relaxed-core all-electron results, PAW dimer bond lengths differ by less than 0.1 percent at a 700 eV cutoff, 0.2 percent for F2, and generally by less than 0.5 percent with 400 eV datasets (Kresse 1999). For diamond, Si, V, Ca and CaF2 the lattice constants, bulk moduli and cohesive energies agree with earlier PAW and FLAPW results within 0.5, 5 and 1 percent. In Fe, Co and Ni atoms the PAW interconfigurational and magnetic energies differ from all-electron values by at most 10 meV. The PAW transition pressure from FM bcc to NM hcp Fe is 11 GPa. For the bcc Fe magnetic energy the standard ultrasoft potential is off by 50 meV in the LDA and overestimates it by 120 meV in the GGA.
