What MACE is

MACE is a family of machine-learned interatomic potentials built on higher-order equivariant message passing. The MACE-MP-0 foundation models, trained on Materials Project trajectories, give energies, forces and stresses across the periodic table without system-specific training. On the map it serves the quantities those models predict.

In more detail

Many-body equivariant message passing

MACE writes each message as a body-order expansion, Eq. 7 of Batatia 2022, and builds the many-body terms from a tensor product of two-body atomic basis features on each node, Eq. 10. The generalised Clebsch-Gordan coefficients that symmetrise the product are sparse and precomputed. The message is a linear combination of these features with element-dependent weights, the update is linear with a residual term, and only the last readout is a one-layer MLP. With correlation order 3 the messages are four-body, and the accuracy on 3BPA stops improving after two layers. The species dimension is left out of the tensor product, so cost does not grow with the number of elements.

Numerics and scale

MACE evaluates the expensive equivariant tensor product on edges once, in the second layer. On an A100 the force latency per structure is 10.5 ms for the invariant model and 24.3 ms for L=2, against 101 to 104 ms for NequIP and BOTNet in the same code. The 3BPA model reaches a converged BOTNet accuracy after about 30 minutes of training (Batatia 2022). MACE-MP-0 was trained for 100 epochs on 40 to 80 H100 GPUs, about 2,600 GPU hours, and reaches 18 meV/atom energy and 39 meV/A force MAE on MPtrj. One A100 runs several nanoseconds per day for 1000 atoms, and domain decomposition scales at 0.1 ns/day up to 32,000 atoms on 64 GPUs (Batatia 2025).

Interfaces and training data

ASE, OpenMM and LAMMPS evaluate trained models, and a JAX version runs inside JAX-MD at 2 to 50 million MD steps per day depending on model size (Kovács 2023). MACE-MP-0 is trained on MPtrj, the dataset compiled for CHGNet from Materials Project relaxations, with PBE and Hubbard U on some transition metal oxides and no dispersion. An additive DFT-D3 term with Becke-Johnson damping restores dispersion for water, layered solids and frameworks. Fine-tuning uses multi-head replay, which keeps a subset of MPtrj in the loss so the model does not forget the foundation data (Batatia 2025).

Validation

On rMD17 the model is at or below the best published errors, for example 6.6 meV/A force MAE on aspirin. On 3BPA at 1200 K it improves on NequIP and Allegro by about 30% (Batatia 2022). On the M3GNet 89-element dataset MACE 128-2 reaches 34.1 meV/atom and 60.1 meV/A against 34.7 and 71.7 for M3GNet, and on HME21 it improves on NequIP and TeaNet by more than 30% (Kovács 2023). MACE-MP-0b3 reproduces the relative energies of 12 ice polymorphs to about 5 meV against PBE-D3, and fine-tuning on five single-point DFT energies per reaction profile brings nudged elastic band barriers on Cu and In2O3 into agreement with DFT (Batatia 2025).
