What MatGL is

MatGL implements graph neural network models for materials. Two of them are the M3GNet universal interatomic potential and CHGNet, which also predicts magnetic moments. The models are pretrained on Materials Project data and run as ASE calculators. On the map it serves the quantities those models predict.

In more detail

Many-body graph networks

M3GNet adds atomic coordinates and the lattice matrix to the materials graph so that forces and stresses can be taken as derivatives. Each bond feature is updated from the other bonds at the same atom, with the angle expanded in spherical Bessel functions and spherical harmonics and a cutoff function that vanishes smoothly with its first two derivatives (Chen 2022). CHGNet keeps an atom graph and a bond graph, updates atom, bond and angle features in each interaction block, and projects the atom features after three blocks onto site magnetic moments. The last block then predicts energy from features that already carry charge-state information (Deng 2023).

Numerics and scale

M3GNet uses a 5 A radial cutoff, a 4 A three-body cutoff, 64-dimensional embeddings and three main blocks, 227,549 weights in all, trained with a Huber loss on energy, forces and stress with weights 1, 1 and 0.1. Its test MAEs are 0.035 eV/atom, 0.072 eV/A and 0.41 GPa. A relaxation of K57Se34 took 22 s on one CPU core against 15 hours on 32 cores for DFT (Chen 2022). CHGNet has 400,438 parameters, a 5 A atom-graph and 3 A bond-graph cutoff, and reaches 30 meV/atom, 77 meV/A, 0.348 GPa and 0.032 muB on the MPtrj test set of 157,955 structures. Four atom convolution layers give it a 20 A receptive field (Deng 2023).

Training data and interfaces

Both models were built on Materials Project data. M3GNet used the MPF.2021.2.8 snapshot of GGA and GGA+U relaxations, CHGNet the MPtrj dataset parsed from the September 2022 database with the GGA/GGA+U mixing correction applied. CHGNet is implemented in PyTorch with structure handling from pymatgen, relaxation and MD through the ASE interface, and cluster expansions through smol (Deng 2023). The same architecture without lattice inputs works as a molecular force field on MD17, and with an intensive readout as a property model on Matbench (Chen 2022).

Validation

M3GNet reproduces phonon dispersions of three SiO2 polymorphs and phonon DOS centres of 1,521 materials with an MAE of 44.2 cm-1, falling to 28.8 cm-1 after a constant shift. Its relaxed volumes bring the median volume error of test structures from 2.4% to 0.6% (Chen 2022). CHGNet was the best of nine models on Matbench Discovery at the time of writing. It agrees with ab initio MD on room-temperature conductivities of Li superionic conductors and infers the same tetrahedral Mn valences as GGA+U at 0.4 ns and 1.5 ns of the LiMnO2 trajectory. Fine-tuning on Li-Fe-P-O structures lowers its test error from 23 to 15 meV/atom (Deng 2023).
